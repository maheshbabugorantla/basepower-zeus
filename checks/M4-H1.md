## Method

For each of the five permitting authorities (City of Austin, unincorporated
Travis County, City of Houston, unincorporated Harris County, State of Texas /
TDLR), I fetched primary or official-guidance pages (city/county code
documents, TDLR's own compliance guide and rulemaking notices, Austin Energy's
published permitting manual, a Houston Permitting Center technical memo
reproducing the adopted fire-code section, and up.codes' jurisdiction viewers
which reproduce each jurisdiction's adopted local text and catalog metadata)
and recorded only facts with a short verbatim excerpt from a page I actually
retrieved.

`WebFetch`'s built-in summarizer paraphrases, so it was used only for initial
orientation. Every quote that ended up in the CSV was re-verified by
re-fetching the same URL with `curl` (or re-extracting the PDF with `pypdf`),
saving the raw text to a local file, and running an automated checker
(`url_map.py`, kept in the session scratchpad) that maps each CSV row's
`source_url` to its own saved raw-text file and asserts every `...`-delimited
piece of the `quote` column is a literal substring of that specific file (not
just "found somewhere"). This caught and fixed three real defects during
review: two rows had quotes swapped between the Texas Register page and
TDLR's news-release page (a sentence unique to one had been attributed to the
other), a `§73.100` section-symbol mismatch (I had written "Sec. 73.100" where
the source uses "§"), and a numbering artifact in the Houston PDF ("1.40 kWh"
is the source's own list-item "1." glued to "40 kWh" with no space — kept
verbatim with an explanatory note rather than "fixed" into something the
source doesn't literally say). Of 25 final rows, 24 are confirmed this way
against a locally saved raw-text copy of their own cited page; the 25th
(Harris County's 2009 order PDF) is a scanned, non-OCR PDF that `pypdf` cannot
extract — it was read and quoted via direct multimodal inspection of the
rendered PDF pages, not grep, which is noted below.

The same review pass also caught a staleness bug: my first draft cited
Austin's *2021*-cycle Fire Code for the residential ESS kWh limits, but
Austin's `up.codes` catalog shows an active *2024* IFC (Ordinance
20250410-039, effective July 10 2025) that supersedes it — the CSV was
corrected to cite and quote the 2024-cycle text instead (which added
"basements" to the 40 kWh tier and renumbered the permits subsection from
1207.1.2 to 1207.1.4, both real differences from the 2021 text I'd
initially quoted). I separately checked Houston's catalog for the same kind
of staleness: `up.codes/codes/houston`'s JSON shows Houston Electrical Code
2023 and Houston Fire Code 2021 both carry `"isActive":true` and
`"effectiveEndDate":null` with no newer (2024- or 2026-cycle) entries in the
catalog at all, so the original Houston facts are not stale.

A second review pass (after the first set of fixes) caught three more scope
issues, all now corrected: (a) the Austin ESS-limits quote had been cut off
before the sentence that actually matters most for Base Core sizing —
"ESS installations exceeding the permitted individual or aggregate ratings
shall be installed in accordance with Sections 1207.1 through 1207.9" (i.e.
an over-limit residential ESS is reclassified under the general ESS rules,
not simply barred) — the quote and value were extended to include it; (b) the
Austin permit row cited the wrong subsection number (1207.1.2, which in the
2024 edition is "Mobile ESS," not "Permits" — the real heading is 1207.1.4);
(c) the Houston permit-required row's value claimed more than its quote
supported (the operational-permit clause) and glossed over Sec. 1207.1's own
exception routing Group R-3/R-4 (residential) ESS to Sec. 1207.11 — both were
fixed by extending the quote and adding an explicit "this is my
interpretation of how the sections interact" caveat to the value.

Two sources returned HTTP 403 on every attempt (Harris County's 2019
"currently adopted codes" PDF on a third-party host, and ICC's
codes.iccsafe.org viewer) and were dropped rather than guessed at; see
Unconfirmed below.

## Open question 6 — has TDLR adopted the 2026 NEC?

**Yes.** TDLR amended 16 TAC §73.100 (Electricians program) to adopt the 2026
National Electrical Code as the statewide electrical code, effective
**September 1, 2026** — it has been in force for about three weeks as of this
check (session date 2026-09-25/26). The rule was filed with the Secretary of
State on August 12, 2026 and its adoption text was published in the Texas
Register's August 28, 2026 issue: "adopts the 2026 National Electrical Code
(NEC) published by the National Fire Protection Association (NFPA) as the
state's electrical code, with one modification," with "Effective date:
September 1, 2026" attached to the same filing (TRD-202603409). TDLR's own
news release corroborates this and specifies the one modification: a limited
GFCI exception for certain outdoor outlets (extending, without expiration, an
exception that would otherwise have expired September 1, 2026).

**Caveat worth flagging:** TDLR's own public-facing Electricians Compliance
Guide (a different, FAQ-style page from the rulemaking notice) still describes
the *2023* NEC as the adopted edition, with no mention of 2026, when I fetched
it directly during this check (2026-09-26). The formal rule (Texas Register /
TDLR news release) and the public compliance guide are inconsistent right now;
treat the Texas Register adopted-rules text as authoritative and the
compliance-guide page as stale/pending an update.

## Key findings worth flagging to M4 pipeline authors

- **Houston and Austin both have jurisdiction-specific, independently
  confirmed residential ESS text**, and the numbers are close to each other
  and to the generic IRC R328/NFPA 855 figures cited in the spec: their
  adopted Fire Code §1207.11 (Group R-3/R-4 occupancies) caps individual ESS
  units at 20 kWh and aggregate capacity at 40 kWh in utility closets/storage
  spaces (Austin's currently-active 2024-cycle text also adds "basements" to
  this tier) or 80 kWh in garages/accessory structures/outdoors. Houston's
  copy of the text came from a Houston Permitting Center-published consultant
  memo reproducing the adopted section, not the ordinance PDF itself —
  flagged as such in the CSV value.
- **Harris County and Travis County's fire codes reference NFPA 855 for ESS**
  but I could not independently fetch the unamended IFC base text (§1207.11)
  they incorporate by reference — ICC's own viewer returned HTTP 403 on every
  attempt. Treat the Houston/Austin figures as the confirmed reference point;
  Travis's and Harris's own residential ESS kWh limits are **not** separately
  confirmed and are listed under Unconfirmed below, not assumed equal.
- **Unincorporated Travis County and unincorporated Harris County do not
  adopt their own IRC edition.** Both fall under Tex. Loc. Gov't Code ch. 233
  subch. F, which lets a county require new residential construction to
  conform to the IRC edition as of May 1, 2008, or the edition applicable in
  the county seat (Austin for Travis, Houston for Harris). Harris County's
  2009 Commissioners Court order explicitly adopts this subchapter. I found
  no equivalent Travis County order — only Travis County Code Chapter 80,
  which requires the builder to declare (not the county to mandate) the IRC
  version used, on the permit application. That Travis County web page's own
  visible header reads "Travis County Code: Chapter 480 Expired" even though
  the body text calls it "Chapter 80" — the page's numbering/status is
  internally inconsistent, so this fact is flagged as provisional in the CSV.
- **Travis County's Fire Code PDF has its own internal inconsistency.** The
  S3-hosted PDF I cite for "2021 IFC, effective Jan 1 2026" states in its own
  body text "The 2021 Fire Code adopted by the Commissioners Court consists
  of ... the International Fire Code, 2021 Edition," but its table of
  contents still carries the heading "201.064 Amendments to the 2015
  International Fire Code" — an apparent copy/paste leftover from an earlier
  version of the same document. I could not corroborate the adopted edition
  or effective date against a `traviscountytx.gov`-hosted source: the
  official Travis County Code pages (`online.encodeplus.com`) and
  `traviscountyfirerescue.org` are both JavaScript-rendered and returned no
  usable plain text to `curl`. This fact is flagged as provisional in the CSV.
- **Base Power Core (39.2 kWh single unit or 78.4 kWh two-unit pair)** exceeds
  Houston's and Austin's confirmed 20 kWh per-individual-unit cap under
  §1207.11.4 as written. Whether a 39.2 kWh unit is treated as one "ESS unit"
  for permitting purposes is a code-interpretation question the spec already
  flags as an evidence question, not something this ticket resolves — M4's
  installability model should treat the per-unit and aggregate caps as
  evidence inputs, not a hard pass/fail gate.

## Unconfirmed (left out of the CSV)

- **Harris County's currently-adopted NEC edition.** The only source found
  (`ascehouston.starchapter.com/images/FC-CURRENTLY-ADOPTED-CODES-1909.pdf`,
  "as of September 1, 2019") returned HTTP 403 to both `curl` and `WebFetch`.
  Harris County's own current Fire Code Regulations PDF references "NFPA 70"
  generically without stating an edition/year, and Harris County does not
  appear to separately license or inspect electrical work (that authority
  sits with TDLR statewide under Tex. Occ. Code ch. 1305), so no
  county-specific NEC edition fact could be sourced.
- **Unincorporated Travis County's electrical code edition.** No Travis
  County-specific electrical-permitting page was found; electrical work in
  unincorporated Travis County is presumably governed by the TDLR statewide
  NEC (2023, then 2026 effective Sept 1 2026), but I could not find a Travis
  County page stating this directly, so it is not in the CSV.
- **Unincorporated Travis County's and unincorporated Harris County's
  residential ESS kWh limits** (the §1207.11-equivalent table). Both
  counties' adopted fire-code documents reference NFPA 855 generally but only
  reproduce *amended* sections; the base (unamended) IFC text containing the
  actual kWh table is inherited by reference, and I could not fetch ICC's own
  IFC text (codes.iccsafe.org returned 403) to quote it directly. Not
  assumed equal to Houston's/Austin's confirmed figures.
- **Unincorporated Travis County's residential ESS permit requirement**,
  quoted directly from Travis's own Fire Code document, for the same reason
  (base §105.6.5 text inherited by reference, not independently fetchable).
- **A dedicated ESS/battery permit guidance page for Travis County or Harris
  County.** I ran targeted searches restricted to each county's own domains
  (`traviscountytx.gov`, `traviscountyfirerescue.org`,
  `oce.harriscountytx.gov`, `harriscountytx.gov`) and found only generic solar
  and general-permit pages, no ESS-specific guidance page comparable to
  Austin's Solar Permitting Manual or Houston's Jensen Hughes memo.
- **Austin's local amendments to the 2026 NEC.** up.codes' catalog lists an
  "Austin Electrical Code 2026" (NFPA 70, 2026) with an effective start date
  of September 1, 2026 — matching the statewide TDLR date — but its full
  amended text was not yet published/viewable on up.codes at retrieval time
  (`isActive: false`, no viewer link), so Austin-specific amendments to the
  2026 NEC could not be independently confirmed.
- Harris County's "currently adopted" IBC/IRC edition for **commercial**
  construction (only the residential-construction state-law baseline was
  confirmed).
